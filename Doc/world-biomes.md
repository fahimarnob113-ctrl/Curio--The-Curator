# World Biomes

Earth's major biomes, grouped by category, with defining traits and real-world examples.

---

## Terrestrial Biomes

### Forests
1. **Tropical Rainforest** — hot, high rainfall year-round, dense canopy layers (Amazon, Congo Basin, Southeast Asia)
2. **Tropical Seasonal Forest / Monsoon Forest** — wet/dry seasons (parts of India, Southeast Asia)
3. **Temperate Deciduous Forest** — four seasons, trees shed leaves (Eastern US, Europe, East Asia)
4. **Temperate Rainforest** — cool, very high rainfall (Pacific Northwest, New Zealand, southern Chile)
5. **Taiga / Boreal Forest** — cold, coniferous, world's largest land biome (Siberia, Canada, Scandinavia)
6. **Mediterranean Forest / Woodland (Chaparral)** — hot dry summers, mild wet winters (California, Mediterranean Basin, parts of Australia/Chile)

### Grasslands
7. **Tropical Savanna** — warm year-round, wet/dry seasons, scattered trees (African Serengeti, parts of Australia/Brazil)
8. **Temperate Grassland / Steppe / Prairie** — hot summers, cold winters, few trees (Great Plains, Eurasian Steppe, Pampas)

### Deserts
9. **Hot Desert** — extreme heat, very low rainfall (Sahara, Arabian, Sonoran)
10. **Cold Desert** — low rainfall, cold temperatures (Gobi, parts of Antarctica, Great Basin)
11. **Semi-Arid Desert** — slightly more rainfall than hot deserts (parts of the American Southwest)
12. **Coastal Desert** — fog-influenced, mild temps (Atacama, Namib)

### Tundra
13. **Arctic Tundra** — permafrost, extremely cold, low vegetation (northern Canada, Siberia, Alaska)
14. **Alpine Tundra** — high-altitude, similar cold conditions at elevation instead of latitude (Andes, Himalayas, Rockies)

### Other Terrestrial
15. **Wetlands (freshwater)** — marshes, swamps, bogs, fens (Everglades, Pantanal, Okavango Delta)
16. **Mangrove Forest** — coastal, saltwater-tolerant trees (Sundarbans, Southeast Asia, Florida)
17. **Chaparral / Shrubland** — dense drought-resistant shrubs (California, South Africa's Fynbos)
18. **Montane / Alpine Forest** — high-elevation coniferous forest below the treeline (Rockies, Alps, Himalayas)

---

## Aquatic Biomes

### Freshwater
19. **Rivers & Streams (Lotic systems)**
20. **Lakes & Ponds (Lentic systems)**
21. **Freshwater Wetlands**

### Marine
22. **Ocean Open Water (Pelagic Zone)**
23. **Coral Reefs** — high biodiversity, warm shallow tropical waters (Great Barrier Reef, Coral Triangle)
24. **Estuaries** — where freshwater meets seawater (Chesapeake Bay, Amazon River mouth)
25. **Deep Sea / Abyssal Zone** — cold, high pressure, no sunlight
26. **Intertidal Zone** — coastal area between high and low tide
27. **Kelp Forests** — cold coastal waters (California coast, Pacific Northwest)
28. **Polar Marine / Ice Ecosystems** — Arctic and Antarctic waters

---

## Extreme / Special Biomes
29. **Polar Ice Caps** — permanent ice, minimal life (Antarctica, Greenland, Arctic Ocean ice)
30. **High Alpine Zones** — above the treeline, extreme cold/altitude
31. **Cave / Subterranean Biomes** — no sunlight, specialized cave-adapted life
32. **Hydrothermal Vent Ecosystems** — deep ocean, chemosynthesis-based life instead of photosynthesis
33. **Volcanic / Geothermal Biomes** — extremophile life near hot springs and vents (Yellowstone, Iceland)
34. **Urban Biome** — increasingly studied as a distinct human-made ecological zone

---

## Quick Reference by Climate Driver
- **Temperature-driven:** Tundra, Taiga, Alpine zones
- **Precipitation-driven:** Rainforest vs. Desert (opposite extremes)
- **Seasonality-driven:** Savanna, Monsoon Forest, Mediterranean biome
- **Salinity-driven (aquatic):** Freshwater vs. Marine vs. Estuarine
- **Light-driven (aquatic):** Pelagic/Photic zone vs. Deep Sea/Aphotic zone
