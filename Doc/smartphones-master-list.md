# Smartphones & Feature Phones — Popular & Controversial (2004–2024)

A two-decade sweep, organized by era. "Controversial" entries are marked with a short note on why.

---

## Feature Phone Era (2004–2008)

- **Nokia 3310 / 3310 reissue** — the "unbreakable" icon
- **Motorola RAZR V3** — defined flip-phone fashion for years
- **Nokia N-Gage** — controversial: a phone/handheld hybrid widely mocked for its "taco" side-talking design and weak game library
- **BlackBerry 8700 / Bold series** — the corporate-email standard before smartphones went mainstream
- **Sony Ericsson Walkman phones (W800i, W810i)** — music-focused, pre-iPhone
- **Motorola ROKR E1** — controversial: Apple's first iTunes phone collaboration, widely panned as limited and buggy, seen as a precursor that pushed Apple to build the iPhone itself
- **LG Chocolate (VX8500)** — a fashion-phone hit, touch-sensitive keys
- **Palm Treo 650/700 series** — early smartphone/PDA hybrid

## The iPhone / Android Launch Era (2007–2010)

- **Apple iPhone (2007)** — controversial at launch: no physical keyboard, no 3G, AT&T exclusivity, no App Store until 2008; derided by rivals (Ballmer's famous dismissal) before reshaping the industry
- **Apple iPhone 3G / 3GS** — brought App Store, cemented the platform
- **HTC Dream / T-Mobile G1 (2008)** — first commercial Android phone
- **BlackBerry Storm (2008)** — controversial: Verizon's rushed touchscreen BlackBerry, widely panned for a laggy, unreliable "SurePress" screen
- **Palm Pre (2009)** — webOS debut, critically loved but commercially failed
- **Motorola Droid (2009)** — Android's first mainstream breakout via Verizon

## Smartphone Boom (2010–2014)

- **Apple iPhone 4** — controversial: "Antennagate," signal loss when held a certain way, Steve Jobs' "you're holding it wrong" response
- **Samsung Galaxy S II / S III** — Samsung's rise as Apple's main rival
- **Nokia Lumia 900/920 (Windows Phone)** — critically praised, commercially marginal
- **BlackBerry Z10 / BB10 launch** — controversial: BlackBerry's late, underwhelming pivot to touchscreen as it lost market share to iOS/Android
- **HTC One (M7)** — praised for build quality, lost the marketing war to Samsung
- **Samsung Galaxy Note (original, 2011)** — controversial at launch: mocked as a "phablet," too big — then set the trend every phone maker followed
- **Amazon Fire Phone (2014)** — controversial flop: 3D "Dynamic Perspective" gimmick, Amazon-locked ecosystem, discontinued within a year

## Peak Smartphone Wars (2014–2018)

- **Apple iPhone 6 / 6 Plus** — controversial: "Bendgate," reports of the phone bending in pockets
- **Samsung Galaxy Note 7 (2016)** — highly controversial: battery fires led to a full global recall and permanent discontinuation, one of the biggest product-safety failures in tech history
- **Apple iPhone 7** — controversial: removed the headphone jack, sparked years of debate
- **Google Pixel (original, 2016)** — Google's first fully in-house phone, replacing Nexus
- **OnePlus One / OnePlus 3** — "flagship killer" positioning, invite-only launch model
- **Essential Phone (2017)** — controversial: Andy Rubin's high-profile startup phone, praised design but plagued by production delays and poor camera; company folded in 2020
- **BlackBerry KEYone / KEY2** — nostalgia play for physical-keyboard holdouts, niche success

## Notch Era, Foldables Emerge (2018–2020)

- **Apple iPhone X** — introduced Face ID and the notch, $999 price controversial at the time
- **Samsung Galaxy Fold (2019)** — controversial: review units broke within days (screens peeling/cracking), delayed launch by months
- **Huawei Mate 30 Pro / P30 Pro** — controversial: hit by the US trade ban cutting off Google Mobile Services, reshaping Huawei's global strategy
- **Google Pixel 4** — controversial: heavily criticized for weak battery life and dropped the fingerprint sensor for face unlock
- **Motorola Razr (2019 foldable revival)** — nostalgia reboot, mixed reviews on durability
- **Xiaomi Mi series** — rapid rise as a budget-flagship alternative, especially outside the US

## Recent Era (2021–2024)

- **Samsung Galaxy Z Fold3/4/5, Z Flip series** — foldables go mainstream
- **Apple iPhone 13/14/15 Pro** — Dynamic Island, USB-C switch (2023, controversial as EU-regulation-driven, not Apple's choice)
- **Apple iPhone 14 Pro "Dynamic Island"** — replaced the notch
- **Nothing Phone (1) / (2)** — controversial/buzzy: transparent back, LED "Glyph" interface, heavy hype-marketing from ex-OnePlus founder Carl Pei
- **Google Pixel 6/7/8 (Tensor chips)** — Google's push into custom silicon, mixed thermal/performance reception
- **Samsung Galaxy S23 Ultra / S24 Ultra** — AI feature push ("Galaxy AI"), controversial marketing after AI-generated moon photos were revealed to be partly synthetic
- **Huawei Mate 60 Pro (2023)** — controversial: surprise 5G-capable chip despite US sanctions, seen as a major geopolitical/tech story
- **CMF Phone 1 (Nothing sub-brand)** — budget transparent-design phone, notable for user-replaceable back panels

---

## Notably Controversial Moments (cross-brand)
- **Apple "Batterygate" (2017)** — Apple admitted to throttling older iPhones with degraded batteries without clear disclosure, leading to lawsuits and a public apology
- **Samsung Galaxy Note 7 recall (2016)** — banned from flights worldwide
- **Huawei US ban (2019–ongoing)** — cut off from Google services and, later, advanced chip supply chains
- **Right-to-repair fights** — Apple, Samsung, and Google all faced criticism over repairability scores and parts pairing
- **USB-C mandate (EU, 2022–2024)** — forced Apple's switch away from Lightning

---

## Notable Feature Phones Still Sold/Relevant in the Smartphone Era
- **Nokia 3310 (2017 reissue)** — nostalgia marketing hit
- **Jio Phone (India, 2017)** — controversial/disruptive: nearly free 4G feature phone that reshaped India's mobile market
- **Light Phone / Light Phone II** — "dumbphone" movement, minimalist marketing aimed at digital detox
- **Punkt MP02** — premium minimalist phone targeting the same audience
