# Anime & Manga — Eras and Iconic Titles

A chronological sweep of anime/manga history, grouped by era, with the defining titles of each.

---

## Foundational Era (1900s–1950s)
1. **Namakura Gatana (1917)** — among the earliest surviving Japanese animated films
2. **Momotaro's Divine Sea Warriors (1945)** — WWII-era propaganda film, first Japanese animated feature
3. **Astro Boy manga (1952, Osamu Tezuka)** — Tezuka's foundational work, "God of Manga" era begins
4. **Tetsujin 28-go manga (1956)** — precursor to the giant-robot genre

## TV Anime Begins (1960s)
5. **Astro Boy (1963 TV series)** — first major Japanese TV anime series
6. **Kimba the White Lion (1965)**
7. **Speed Racer / Mach GoGoGo (1967)**
8. **GeGeGe no Kitaro (1968)**
9. **Star of the Giants (1968)** — early sports anime

## Early Growth & Genre Formation (1970s)
10. **Mazinger Z (1972)** — defined the piloted-giant-robot (mecha) genre
11. **Devilman (1972)**
12. **Space Battleship Yamato (1974)** — credited with sparking the "anime boom" fanbase
13. **Heidi, Girl of the Alps (1974)** — Miyazaki/Takahata early work
14. **Mobile Suit Gundam (1979)** — launched the "real robot" mecha subgenre, massive franchise start
15. **Galaxy Express 999 (1978)**
16. **Doraemon manga (1969) / anime (1973, 1979)**

## The 1980s — OVA Boom & Global Sci-Fi Wave
17. **Urusei Yatsura (1981)**
18. **Macross (1982)**
19. **Fist of the North Star (1983)**
20. **Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind (1984, Miyazaki)**
21. **Dragon Ball manga (1984) / anime (1986)**
22. **Saint Seiya (1986)**
23. **Grave of the Fireflies (1988, Studio Ghibli)**
24. **My Neighbor Totoro (1988, Studio Ghibli)**
25. **Akira (1988)** — landmark for international anime recognition
26. **Ranma ½ (1987 manga)**

## The 1990s — Golden Age / Shonen Jump Explosion
27. **Sailor Moon (1992)**
28. **Dragon Ball Z (1989–1996)**
29. **Slam Dunk (1990 manga)**
30. **Neon Genesis Evangelion (1995)** — reshaped the mecha genre and anime storytelling broadly
31. **Yu Yu Hakusho (1990 manga)**
32. **Rurouni Kenshin (1994 manga)**
33. **Cowboy Bebop (1998)**
34. **One Piece manga (1997)** — begins what becomes the best-selling manga series of all time
35. **Pokémon anime (1997)**
36. **Cardcaptor Sakura (1996 manga)**
37. **Berserk manga (1989)**
38. **Princess Mononoke (1997, Studio Ghibli)**
39. **Trigun (1998)**
40. **Initial D manga (1995)**

41. **Naruto manga (1999) / anime (2002)**
42. **Bleach manga (2001)**
43. **Fullmetal Alchemist (2001 manga / 2003 & 2009 anime)**
44. **Death Note (2003 manga)**

53. **Sword Art Online (2012)**
54. **One Punch Man (2012 webcomic)**
55. **Your Lie in April (2011 manga)**

63. **Jujutsu Kaisen (2018 manga / 2020 anime)**
64. **Chainsaw Man (2018 manga / 2022 anime)**
65. **Spy x Family (2019 manga)**

68. **Oshi no Ko (2020 manga)**

## Long-Running Shonen "Big Three" (and successors)
- **Naruto, One Piece, Bleach** — the defining "Big Three" of 2000s Weekly Shonen Jump
- **My Hero Academia, Demon Slayer, Jujutsu Kaisen** — often cited as the modern successor "Big Three"

## Notable Controversial/Landmark Moments
- **Pokémon "Electric Soldier Porygon" episode (1997)** — caused hundreds of seizure hospitalizations in Japan, pulled from rotation permanently
- **Grave of the Fireflies vs. My Neighbor Totoro double-billing (1988)** — infamous tonal mismatch, box office underperformance despite critical acclaim
- **Death Note's global piracy-driven popularity** — one of the earliest anime/manga series to blow up largely through unofficial fan scanlations/subs
- **Attack on Titan's shifting political readings** — themes have been debated/interpreted differently across different countries' audiences
- **Berserk's hiatuses and Kentaro Miura's death (2021)** — manga continued posthumously by his studio/assistants, unusual industry situation
